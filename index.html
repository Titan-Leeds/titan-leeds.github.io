§ III · The name

Reports of our demise have been greatly exaggerated.

Titan Leeds (1699–1738) was a colonial almanack-maker with the misfortune of acquiring a competitor named Benjamin Franklin.

In the very first Poor Richard’s Almanack, Franklin solemnly predicted that his “good friend” Mr. Leeds would die on October 17, 1733, at 3:29 in the afternoon. When the day passed and Leeds protested in print that he was very much alive, Franklin replied that the real Leeds would never be so rude, so he must indeed be dead and his almanack carried on by an impostor.

It was the best marketing stunt of the eighteenth century, and Leeds kept publishing straight through it. We admire the inventor. We are named for the one who kept showing up to work.
